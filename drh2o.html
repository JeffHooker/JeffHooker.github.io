Dose (mg/L) = Demand (mg/L) + Residual (mg/L) lbs/day = Flow (MGD) × Dose (mg/L) × 8.34

Use when calculating how much chlorine to add to meet a target residual, or how many pounds per day a system must feed.

Source: ABC Need-to-Know Criteria for Operators, Grade 1 Water Treatment

Q = A × V GPM = Area (ft²) × Velocity (fps) × 448.83

Use when calculating how many gallons per minute flow through a pipe given the pipe size and water velocity.

Source: Sacramento State Office of Water Programs — Water Distribution Math

DT (min) = Volume (gal) ÷ Flow Rate (GPM) DT (hr) = Volume (gal) ÷ Flow Rate (GPH)

Use to find how long water remains in a tank or basin — critical for verifying adequate contact time for disinfection (CT).

Source: ABC Need-to-Know Criteria for Operators, Grade 1 Water Treatment

V (gal) = 0.785 × D² × H × 7.48 D = diameter (ft), H = height (ft)

Use to find the capacity of a round storage tank, clearwell, or standpipe in gallons.

Source: Sacramento State Office of Water Programs — Water Treatment Math

V (gal) = L × W × H × 7.48 L = length (ft), W = width (ft), H = depth (ft)

Use to find the capacity of a rectangular basin, sedimentation tank, or storage vault in gallons.

Source: Sacramento State Office of Water Programs — Water Treatment Math

WHP = (GPM × Head ft) ÷ 3,960 BHP = WHP ÷ Pump Efficiency MHP = BHP ÷ Motor Efficiency

Use when sizing a pump motor, calculating energy consumption, or checking pump efficiency on an existing system.

Source: AWWA — Water Transmission and Distribution (Principles and Practices of Water Supply Operations)

Head (ft) = PSI × 2.31 PSI = Head (ft) × 0.433

Use to convert between pressure gauge readings (PSI) and feet of head — needed for pump curves, elevation calculations, and system design.

Source: AWWA — Distribution System Operation and Maintenance

lbs/day = Flow (MGD) × Dose (mg/L) × 8.34 gal/day = lbs/day ÷ (% Strength × 8.34)

Use to calculate how much chemical to feed daily — works for chlorine, fluoride, coagulants, or any treatment chemical.

Source: ABC Need-to-Know Criteria for Operators, Grade 1 Water Treatment

% Strength = (Wt of Chemical ÷ Wt of Solution) × 100

Use to find the concentration of a chemical solution you are mixing or to verify the strength of a delivered product before feeding it.

Source: ABC Need-to-Know Criteria for Operators, Grade 1 Water Treatment

SVI (mL/g) = SSV₃₀ (mL/L) × 1000 ÷ MLSS (mg/L)

Use to assess sludge settleability. Normal range: 80–150 mL/g. Above 150 indicates potential bulking.

Source: Sacramento State OIT Wastewater T1 Study Guide

RAS % = (RAS Flow ÷ Influent Flow) × 100

Typical RAS rate is 25–100% of influent flow. Higher rates increase MLSS in the aeration basin.

Source: Sacramento State OIT Wastewater T1 Study Guide

Inventory (lbs) = Concentration (mg/L) × Volume (MG) × 8.34

Used to calculate total pounds of mixed liquor solids in the aeration basin system.

Source: ABC Need-to-Know Criteria, Grade T1 Wastewater Treatment

SRT (days) = MLSS Inventory (lbs) ÷ Waste Sludge (lbs/day)

Target SRT varies: conventional AS 5–15 days; extended aeration 20–30 days; nitrification ≥ 10 days.

Source: ABC Need-to-Know Criteria, Grade T1 Wastewater Treatment

F/M = BOD Loading (lbs/day) ÷ MLVSS Inventory (lbs)

Conventional AS: 0.2–0.4. Extended aeration: 0.05–0.15. Higher F/M = younger sludge.

Source: Sacramento State OIT Wastewater T1 Study Guide

SOR (gpd/ft²) = Flow (GPD) ÷ Clarifier Surface Area (ft²)

Typical design: 400–800 gpd/ft² for primary clarifiers; 400–600 for secondary. Exceeding limits causes carryover.

Source: ABC Need-to-Know Criteria, Grade T1 Wastewater Treatment

SLR (lbs/ft²/day) = (Q_inf + Q_RAS) × MLSS × 8.34 ÷ Area (ft²)

Secondary clarifier design max: 20–30 lbs/ft²/day. Overloading causes sludge blanket rise and solids loss.

Source: Sacramento State OIT Wastewater T1 Study Guide
